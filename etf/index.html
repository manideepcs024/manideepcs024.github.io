ETFs, simply explained.

An ETF (Exchange Traded Fund) lets you buy a whole basket of stocks, gold or bonds in one click, at a very low cost, through your demat account. One Nifty 50 ETF unit = a tiny slice of all 50 Nifty companies. Here's how they work, the different types, and how to choose one well.

⚙️ How an ETF works

An ETF is a mutual fund that is listed on the stock exchange. You buy and sell it like a share, during market hours, at the live price.

🏦Fund house (AMC)launches an ETF that tracks an index, say Nifty 50.
🧺Basketthe AMC holds the same stocks as the index, in the same weights.
🔁Market makerscreate or redeem units in large blocks, keeping the price close to the basket's value.
📈Exchangeunits trade on NSE/BSE all day; you see a live price and the iNAV.
🙋Youbuy from your demat account, hold as long as you want, sell any trading day.

📏 NAV vs market price vs iNAV

  • NAV: value of the basket, calculated once a day after the close.
  • iNAV (indicative NAV): the basket's value updated every few seconds during the day. Shown on the AMC/NSE website.
  • Market price: what buyers and sellers are paying right now. In a liquid ETF it stays very close to iNAV.

📐 Tracking error & difference

  • Tracking difference: how much the ETF's return lagged the index over a year (mostly its costs).
  • Tracking error: how jumpy that gap is. Lower is better for both.
  • A good Nifty 50 ETF trails the index by only a small fraction of a percent a year.

✅ Why ETFs are better for many investors

  • Very low cost: no expensive fund manager; the saving compounds for decades.
  • No fund-manager risk: it follows the index, so there's no bad stock-picking.
  • Instant diversification: 50 or 100 companies in one unit.
  • Transparent: you know exactly what it holds.
  • Flexible: buy or sell any time the market is open, no exit load.
  • Easy asset mix: equity, gold, silver and bonds all from one demat account.

⚠️ What to watch out for

  • Liquidity: low-volume ETFs can have a wide gap between buy and sell prices.
  • Premium / discount: in a rush, the price can trade well above iNAV. Don't overpay.
  • Brokerage & demat charges apply, unlike a direct mutual fund.
  • Sector & thematic ETFs are concentrated and volatile; they're not a core holding.
  • International ETFs can trade at big premiums when overseas investment limits are hit.
  • An ETF follows the index down too; it's not a safe-return product.

🛒 How to buy an ETF well

Pick the index first, then the ETF: for a core holding, Nifty 50 or Sensex; add Nifty Next 50 or a midcap index for more growth.
Compare ETFs on the same index: lower expense ratio, lower tracking error, and higher daily traded volume.
Check price vs iNAV on the AMC or NSE website; avoid buying at a big premium.
Use a limit order near iNAV, never a market order, especially in the first and last 15 minutes of the day.
Buy regularly: a fixed amount every month works like a SIP. Many brokers offer ETF SIPs.
Hold for the long term and rebalance once a year between equity, gold and debt.

💰 Tax (simplified): equity ETFs are taxed like equity shares (short-term if sold within 12 months, long-term after that, with the yearly long-term exemption). Gold, silver, debt and international ETFs follow different rules, which have changed in recent Budgets. Use the tax estimator for equity, and check current rules for others.
