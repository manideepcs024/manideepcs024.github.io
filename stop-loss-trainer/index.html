How it works
A good stop sits just below the recent swing low (where the trade idea is clearly wrong) and gives the price enough room for its normal daily range (ATR). Then you size the position so that stop costs only ~1% of capital.

📚 How to place a stop-loss

1. Find the invalidationAsk: "At what price is my idea clearly wrong?" For a long trade, that's usually below the most recent swing low or below the breakout level.
2. Give it room (ATR)ATR = the stock's average daily range. A stop closer than ~1× ATR gets hit by normal noise. Many traders use 1–2× ATR below entry or below the swing low.
3. Then size the positionStop distance decides quantity, not the other way round: Qty = (capital × 1%) ÷ (entry − stop). A wider stop just means fewer shares.
4. Too far? Skip itIf the logical stop is 4–5× ATR away, the reward-to-risk is usually poor. Wait for a better entry closer to support.
5. Never widen itMoving a stop further away "to give it room" after entry is how small losses become big ones. Only trail it in your favour.
6. Accept stop-outsEven perfect stops get hit sometimes. That's the cost of doing business. A small loss is a successful stop-loss.
