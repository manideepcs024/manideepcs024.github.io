📞 CE & PE: the four basic positions

An option is a contract. CE (call) = the right to buy at the strike price. PE (put) = the right to sell at the strike price. The buyer pays a premium and has a right; the seller (writer) collects the premium and has an obligation.

🎚️ ITM, ATM, OTM and what the premium is made of

Premium = Intrinsic value + Time value

Intrinsic = how much the option is worth if it expired right now (call: spot − strike; put: strike − spot; never below 0). Time value = what people pay for the chance of a move before expiry. Time value always becomes zero at expiry.

⏳ Time decay (theta): the option buyer's enemy

Every day that passes without a move, the time value melts. It melts slowly at first and very fast in the last week. Weekends and holidays count too: the market prices them in. This is why most option buyers lose even when they get the direction right, but late.

🛡️ Hedging strategies vs naked positions

Hedging means taking a second position that gains when your main position loses, like insurance. You give up a little profit to cap the damage. Naked means no protection at all. Pick a strategy and compare the payoff at expiry (uses the inputs above).

☠️ Naked F&O: why it blows up accounts

Naked futuresNo stop, no hedge. A 5% gap down on a ₹5 lakh futures position is ₹25,000 gone at the open, before you can react. Leverage cuts both ways.
Naked option sellingYou earn small, steady premiums for weeks, then one big move (results, budget, war news) can wipe out months of income. The loss on a naked call has no ceiling.
Buying cheap OTM optionsNot 'naked' risk-wise, but ₹2 options usually expire at ₹0. Low price ≠ low risk of losing all of it.
Margin callsWhen a naked position moves against you, the broker asks for more margin or squares you off at the worst moment.
The dataSEBI's studies of individual F&O traders found that roughly 9 out of 10 lost money. Learn on paper, start small, and always define your maximum loss.
The fixDefine risk before entry: a stop-loss, a hedge leg (spread), or a position small enough that the worst case doesn't hurt. Plan it in the Option Selling Planner.

📘 Quick glossary

StrikeThe fixed price in the contract.
ExpiryThe last day the contract trades. After that, it's settled and gone.
Lot sizeOptions trade in fixed lots (e.g. 500 shares). Premium × lot = what you pay.
IV (implied volatility)How big a move the market expects. High IV = expensive options (before results, events).
DeltaHow much the premium moves for ₹1 move in the stock. ATM ≈ 0.5.
ThetaValue lost per day from time decay.
Physical settlementStock F&O held into expiry is settled by actual delivery of shares. ITM stock options can mean buying/selling the full lot in cash.
Open interest (OI)Number of open contracts. Rising OI + rising price = fresh buying.
