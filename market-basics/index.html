🏦 Demat, trading account, broker & DP

You need three linked accounts. Most brokers open the demat and trading accounts together in one online process (with PAN, Aadhaar and a bank account).

🏛️ Demat accountHolds your shares and ETFs electronically, like a bank account for securities. Shares sit with a depository (NSDL or CDSL), in your name.
📲 Trading accountUsed to place buy and sell orders on the exchange. It connects your bank account (money) with your demat account (shares).
🧑‍💼 BrokerA SEBI-registered member of the exchange who sends your orders: discount brokers (low flat fees, app-based) or full-service brokers (research, advice, higher fees).
🗄️ Depository Participant (DP)The agent between you and NSDL/CDSL; usually your broker. A small DP charge applies when shares leave your demat (when you sell).
🏦 Bank accountFunds move in and out through your linked bank account. Money is credited after settlement.
🧾 Charges on every tradeBrokerage, STT, exchange fees, SEBI fee, stamp duty and GST. Small per trade, big for frequent traders.

🔁 The journey of an order

🙋Youplace a buy order on the broker's app.
🏢ExchangeNSE/BSE matches it with a seller at your price or better.
🧮Clearing corp.guarantees the trade and settles money and shares.
📥Your dematshares arrive on T+1 (the next working day).
⏰ Market timings (equity): pre-open 9:00–9:15 AM (orders collected, opening price found), normal trading 9:15 AM – 3:30 PM, closing session shortly after. T+1 settlement: buy on Monday, shares in your demat on Tuesday; sell on Monday, money on Tuesday.

🧾 Order types

Market orderBuys or sells immediately at the best available price. Fast, but you can get a bad price in thin or fast markets.USE RARELY
Limit orderBuys or sells only at your price or better. You control the price; it may not fill.DEFAULT CHOICE
Stop-loss (SL) orderSits waiting; when the price hits your trigger, it becomes a limit order. Protects a position automatically.
SL-Market (SL-M)When triggered, becomes a market order: sure exit, uncertain price. Not allowed in every segment (e.g. options at many brokers).
GTT (Good Till Triggered)A broker feature that waits for weeks or months until your price is hit, then places the order. Great for swing stop-losses and targets.
AMO (After Market Order)Placed after hours or before the open; sent to the exchange when the market opens.

🏭 SME stocks vs Mainboard stocks

Small and medium enterprises list on separate platforms: NSE Emerge and BSE SME. They can grow fast, but the rules and the risks are very different.

MainboardSME platform
Company sizeLarger, establishedSmall, often young businesses
How you buyAny quantity (even 1 share)Only in lots (a fixed minimum quantity, often worth ₹1 lakh+)
IPO minimumOne lot, usually ~₹15,000Much higher minimum application
ResultsEvery quarterOften half-yearly only
LiquidityUsually goodOften thin: hard to sell in a fall
RiskNormal market riskHigher: easier to manipulate; SEBI has flagged price rigging in some SME stocks
🚩 Watch out: huge SME IPO subscriptions and listing pops attract hype. Check the business, the promoters, related-party transactions and whether you could exit if buyers disappear. Use the FOMO detector on SME "tips".

🚧 Upper circuit, lower circuit & price bands

Exchanges limit how far many stocks can move in a day, using price bands of 2%, 5%, 10% or 20% from the previous close. Stocks in F&O don't have fixed bands; they use dynamic limits instead.

📈 Upper circuit (UC)The price hits the top of the band: only buyers, almost no sellers. Your buy order may not fill at all. Chasing UC stocks is a common trap.
📉 Lower circuit (LC)Only sellers, no buyers. You can't sell: you're stuck until buyers return, sometimes for days. That's the real danger of hyped small stocks.
🌐 Market-wide circuit breakerIf the Nifty or Sensex falls (or rises) 10%, 15% or 20% in a day, trading in the whole market halts for a while.

🔒 BE / trade-to-trade, ASM & GSM

Exchanges move some stocks into special segments or surveillance to cool down speculation. These change how you can trade them.

🔒 BE series / Trade-to-Trade (T2T)On NSE you'll see BE instead of EQ (BSE: "T" group). Every trade must be settled by delivery: no intraday. If you buy and sell the same day, both are settled separately, and you can end up with a short-delivery penalty.
👁️ ASM (Additional Surveillance Measure)For stocks with unusual price or volume moves. Can mean higher margins (up to 100%), tighter price bands or a move to trade-to-trade.
🧯 GSM (Graded Surveillance Measure)For small companies whose price looks out of line with their fundamentals. Stages add restrictions such as 100% margin, extra deposits and limited trading frequency.
🔤 Other series you'll seeEQ: normal stocks. BZ: companies not meeting listing rules. SM / ST: SME stocks. Always check the series before buying.
Why it matters: stocks under ASM/GSM or in BE are usually being watched for a reason. Liquidity is thin, intraday is not allowed and exits can be hard. Beginners should simply avoid them.

⛔ F&O ban period

Each F&O stock has a market-wide position limit (MWPL): the total open interest allowed across all traders. When open interest crosses 95% of MWPL, the stock enters the ban period.

What changesNo new F&O positions can be created in that stock. You can only reduce or close existing positions. Increasing a position can attract penalties.
When it endsWhen open interest falls below 80% of MWPL, the ban is lifted. NSE publishes the ban list every day.
Cash market?Buying or selling the shares (CNC/MIS) continues as normal. Only the derivatives are restricted.
What it tells youA stock in ban has a very crowded derivatives position, so it can be volatile. It's a signal to be careful, not a trade by itself.
