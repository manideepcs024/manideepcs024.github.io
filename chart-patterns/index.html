Chart patterns, drawn and decoded.

Patterns are pictures of supply and demand: where buyers gave up, where sellers ran out, where price coiled before a move. Learn to draw trendlines first, then each pattern with its entry, stop-loss and target.

✏️ How to draw a trendline (and support/resistance)

Find swing points. In an uptrend, mark the clear swing lows; in a downtrend, the swing highs.
Connect two of them and extend the line to the right. Use wicks or bodies, but be consistent.
A third touch confirms it. The more touches (and the longer the line), the more it matters.
Don't force it. If you must cut through many candles to make it fit, it's not a valid line. Slightly angled lines (30–45°) last longer than very steep ones.
Support & resistance are horizontal zones where price turned several times. Once broken, old resistance often becomes new support (role reversal), and a retest is a classic entry.
A break of the trendline on a strong close (with volume) is the first warning the trend is changing; a lower high after it confirms.

🧭 Rules that make patterns work

📊 Volume confirmsReal breakouts come with volume well above average. A breakout on low volume often fails.
🕯️ Wait for the closeA wick through the neckline isn't a breakout. Wait for a candle to close beyond it (daily for swing trades).
🔁 Retests are giftsPrice often comes back to test the broken level. Entering on a successful retest gives a tighter stop.
📈 Trade with the trendContinuation patterns (flags, cups, VCP) in an uptrend above the 200 EMA have better odds than reversals against it.
❌ Know the failure pointIf price falls back inside the pattern and closes there, the setup has failed: exit, don't hope.
