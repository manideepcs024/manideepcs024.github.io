Every sector, explained.

The Indian market isn't one thing. Banks move on interest rates, cement on monsoons and elections, defence on government orders, IT on the US economy. Learn what drives each sector, what numbers to check and which companies are in it. Tap any sector to open it.

⚔️ Defence sector

Companies that build fighter jets, missiles, warships, radars and ammunition for the armed forces. Driven by government orders, the defence budget and "Make in India". War headlines make these stocks jump, and very volatile.

e.g. HAL · BEL · Mazagon Dock · BDL
≠

🛡️ Defensive sectors

Sectors whose sales don't fall much in a slowdown, because people still buy soap, medicine and mobile data. They fall less in a crash, and usually rise less in a boom.

e.g. FMCG · Pharma · Telecom · Power utilities

🧭 How to use sectors in your trading

Trade the strongest sectorMost of a stock's move comes from its sector and the market. Pick leaders from the 2–3 strongest sectors: compare sector indices against the Nifty 50.
Check the right numbersA bank's P/E means little; use P/B and NPAs. For cement, EBITDA per tonne. For realty, pre-sales. Each sector has its own scorecard (see each card).
Don't over-concentrateFive stocks from one sector is one bet, not five. Keep a single sector to 25–30% of your portfolio at most.
Themes get crowdedDefence, railways and PSU stocks ran hard on news and budgets. When everyone talks about a theme, valuations are usually stretched. Size smaller.
Watch the macro triggersInterest rates (banks, realty, auto), crude oil (OMCs, paints, aviation), the rupee (IT, pharma), monsoon (FMCG, agri, tractors).
